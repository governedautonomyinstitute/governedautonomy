# Changelog

The doctrine is versioned, dated, and revised in the open.

A doctrine that asks enterprises to govern autonomous behavior must itself be governed: every substantive change to the Governed Autonomy Doctrine is versioned, dated, and recorded here. The current version is **v4**. Versions v1.0 through v3.2 were published as the *AI Harness Doctrine* at aiharnessdoctrine.org; the historical entries below retain that name because that is the name under which they shipped.

---

## v4 · 1 November 2026

Major revision. Law 2 changes from control during execution to protected enforcement before each effect, with pre-commit rechecks when material conditions can change. The five Laws, five Planes and six Pillars remain the organizing architecture, with revised bodies.

- A mandatory deployment baseline ([R01 to R18](https://governedautonomy.org/doctrine/)) and use-case assurance profiles are separated from organizational maturity.
- Standing bounded authorization, current execution checks, bounded general-purpose tools, federated delegation, cumulative constraints, state and output lineage, intervention, continuity, exceptions and verifiable reconstruction evidence are explicit.
- The [Assessment Companion](https://governedautonomy.org/assessment/) defines applicability, methods, evidence states, claims and retests (R19 to R24).
- R16 retains tamper-evidence and independent verifiability within the declared evidence model. R23 separates independent acceptance of an equivalent control from self-assessment.
- The [Organizational Maturity Evaluation](https://governedautonomy.org/maturity/) replaces the v3.6 Maturity Model: rated by dimension, separate from any deployment, and it licenses nothing.
- Continuous proposal intake replaces the RFC-window process. Feedback is accepted at any time; the Institute reviews it and the Executive Director releases minor versions. A change is major, and issues a new major version, when it alters the text or meaning of a Law, the Planes or Pillars, or the scope of the mandatory runtime baseline. See [how changes are accepted and released](https://governedautonomy.org/about/#how-changes-are-accepted-and-released).
- The public [Proposal Log](https://governedautonomy.org/proposals/) opens, with stable identifiers and a recorded disposition for each entry.
- [RFC 001 revision 2](https://governedautonomy.org/rfc/v4-conformance-layer/) is preserved as a historical proposal, not adopted v4 text.

v3.6 is superseded by v4; it was not ratified.

## v3.6 · September 2026 (superseded by v4; not ratified)

Text revisions following the external critical assessment of v3.5 (September 2026), the Institute's response plan, and a review of both by a council of independent model reviewers on four vendors. Doctrine substance: no change to the names or count of the Laws, Planes or Pillars. Structural changes arising from the same review were planned for an RFC 001 revision 3, which was never published; RFC 001 revision 2 is preserved as a historical proposal.

- [Maturity Model](https://governedautonomy.org/maturity/): a level describes organizational capability and does not license any deployment; every deployment, at every level, satisfies all five Laws for the actions it takes. The sentence declaring the Declaration's gap closed at Level 2 is replaced.
- [The Zero Trust Parallel](https://governedautonomy.org/zero-trust/), the [Declaration](https://governedautonomy.org/) and the [Architecture](https://governedautonomy.org/architecture/) page now state which existing primitives the doctrine builds on (attribute-based access control per NIST SP 800-162; policy decision and enforcement points per NIST SP 800-207) and what it adds. The claim that runtime enforcement "did not previously exist" is withdrawn. Planes 2 and 3 are stated in policy-decision and policy-enforcement terms.
- The [comparison pages](https://governedautonomy.org/vs/) share one closing test for any control: does it mediate consequential actions, and can the governed actor bypass it.
- [Plane 4](https://governedautonomy.org/architecture/) requires a reconstructable, tamper-evident transaction record governed as a data asset, in place of full execution trace logging; Framework Pillar 5 says the same.
- Delegation wording on the Architecture and [Framework](https://governedautonomy.org/framework/) pages now matches Law 4 and RFC 001 C5.2: authority is independently granted at the receiving node, not transferred.
- The [definition page](https://governedautonomy.org/what-is-governed-autonomy/) no longer implies every governed action is reversible.
- [Glossary](https://governedautonomy.org/glossary/): entries added for *plane* and for the doctrine's use of *harness*.
- Published the [Status](https://governedautonomy.org/status/) page (September 15): a dated map of eight constructs placed from most settled to least settled, with the changelog entry, standards page or RFC revision that justifies each placement. Reference page; no doctrine substance.
- Corrections (October 2026): the [NIST AI RMF mapping](https://governedautonomy.org/standards/nist-ai-rmf/) no longer describes the framework as written before agents could act, and drops a closing claim of priority; the [joint agentic AI guidance mapping](https://governedautonomy.org/standards/cisa-agentic-ai/) now carries the guidance's recommendation to limit agentic AI to low-risk, non-sensitive tasks, stops describing it as a control catalogue, and restates practices whose earlier wording was not found in the guidance's text; the [OWASP mapping](https://governedautonomy.org/standards/owasp-agentic-top10/) cites RFC 001 revision 2 (C2.3 and C1.5) instead of a revision 1 criterion; the Zero Trust and IAM comparison wording says what those systems do not do on their own rather than what they cannot do, and the IAM page carries the shared closing test; the Declaration's Intent Hijacking line says behavioral governance checks an action against its mission and declared intent; an unsupported claim on the home page is removed; the [Agentic vs Generative](https://governedautonomy.org/agentic-ai-vs-generative-ai/) comparison no longer treats generative risk as informational only. The Standards Observatory and Standards index entries for both documents are aligned with their mappings, and two claims about the joint guidance that are not in its text are removed. The same priority note is dropped from the OWASP mapping; the SIEM comparison, comparison index, glossary Intent Hijacking entry, Framework Pillar 5 line and Maturity Level 3 line are aligned with the reconstructable record and the narrowed intent wording, and the Agentic vs Generative closing line describes the maturity model as organizational capability. RFC 001 criteria gain per-criterion anchors (for example [#c4-5](https://governedautonomy.org/rfc/v4-conformance-layer/#c4-5)); no criterion text changed. Technical: `/sitemap.xml` redirects to `/sitemap-index.xml`. Doctrine substance unchanged.

## v3.3 · August 6, 2026

- **The doctrine is republished at governedautonomy.org as the Governed Autonomy Doctrine.** The substance is unchanged: the 5 Laws, the 5 Architectural Planes, the 6 Framework Pillars, the Threat Surface, and the Maturity Model carry over intact, with their anchors preserved. Only the name changes.
- Rationale, recorded plainly: across the industry, "harness" now denotes the enablement scaffolding around a model — the semantic inverse of this doctrine's subject — and the former name collided with several unrelated works. "Governed autonomy" is the state the doctrine exists to make reachable, and has been this doctrine's own language since the Maturity Model shipped.
- Publisher renamed accordingly: the **Governed Autonomy Institute**.
- Technical: [llms.txt](https://governedautonomy.org/llms.txt) is now generated from the built site at every deploy (an [llms-full.txt](https://governedautonomy.org/llms-full.txt) with full page text was added); structured data expanded (per-page TechArticle plus a DefinedTermSet for the canonical vocabulary); cache-control headers added so stale page variants cannot be served after a deploy.

## v3.0 · May 11–12, 2026

Major revision of the doctrine's structure and vocabulary.

- **Least Agency** introduced as a first-class principle, the third leg of the governance trilogy (Least Privilege &rarr; Least Trust &rarr; Least Agency) — and landed in Law 1.
- **Law 4 renamed** from "Agent-to-Agent Trust Must Be Explicit" to **"Trust Does Not Travel,"** broadening its scope to every handoff type: delegation, orchestration, tool invocation, and subagent spawning.
- **Architecture expanded from 4 to 5 Planes.** Plane 5 (Multi-Agent Trust & Delegation) added as the architectural home of Law 4; Plane 1 renamed to Agent Identity & Lifecycle; Plane 4 broadened to Human Oversight, Audit & Traceability.
- **Framework expanded to 6 Pillars.** Mission Definition split out from Agent Identity; Multi-Agent Governance added.
- **Threat Surface** section added to the Declaration: prompt injection, intent hijacking, cascading failure, behavioral drift.
- **Maturity Model** published as a standalone page: Identified &rarr; Governed &rarr; Continuous.
- The Declaration's three non-negotiables sharpened; homepage redesigned.
