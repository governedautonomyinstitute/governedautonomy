# How to Cite

The doctrine is versioned so that citations can be exact. Cite the version, and your reference never drifts under you.

## Citing the doctrine

> Governed Autonomy Institute. *The Governed Autonomy Doctrine*, v4 (2026). https://governedautonomy.org/

## Citing a specific Law, Plane, or Pillar

Every Law, Plane, and Pillar carries a stable anchor so external documents can cite doctrine sections directly. Example:

> Governed Autonomy Institute. "Law 4: Trust Does Not Travel." *The Governed Autonomy Doctrine*, v4 (2026). https://governedautonomy.org/laws/#law-4-trust-does-not-travel

Anchors are preserved across versions and across the August 2026 renaming; they are treated as part of the doctrine's public interface, and breaking one would be recorded in the [changelog](https://governedautonomy.org/changelog/) as a substantive change.

## Versioning and continuity

The current version is always named in the footer of every page, and every substantive change is recorded in the [changelog](https://governedautonomy.org/changelog/). Works citing versions v1.0 through v3.2 under the doctrine's former name, the AI Harness Doctrine at aiharnessdoctrine.org, refer to this same doctrine: the former domain permanently redirects here path-for-path, and the historical entries in the changelog retain the name under which they shipped.

For readers who need the complete text in one file, to diff a passage or to check what a page said, [llms-full.txt](https://governedautonomy.org/llms-full.txt) carries the full text of every page and is regenerated from the built site at every deploy, so it is never behind the pages it covers.

Proposals under RFC are not doctrine and should be cited as proposals, with their revision number: for example, "RFC 001, revision 2 (August 2026), historical proposal; not adopted v4 text." Nothing on an RFC page is citable as the doctrine's position.
